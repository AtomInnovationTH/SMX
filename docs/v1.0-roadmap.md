# Space Monkey Elevator — v1.0 Roadmap

> Goal: revive the project from "published v0.1.0" to a **polished, legally clean, well-documented public v1.0**.
> This plan supersedes the now-stale `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` and `REVIEW.md`, which describe bugs that are already fixed.
>
> **Reframing (important):** this is not "just a game." It is a **cartoon-wrapped simulation of contactless climbing of a space-elevator seed tether.** The monkey's hands are **electro-permanent magnets (EPMs)** that *pulse* to couple to vertical traveling waves in the tether — no physical contact. The cute monkey exists to keep people open-minded about a genuinely novel propulsion concept. See **Part B** for the simulation-fidelity dimension; the original launch/polish roadmap is now **Part A**.

## Part B — Simulation concept & fidelity (the real point)

### B.0 The real-world mapping (from the author)

| In-game | Real concept |
|---|---|
| The vibrating "vine"/tether | The **first seed tether** of a space elevator |
| The monkey | A **climber** ferrying material *up* to thicken/strengthen the seed tether |
| Monkey's hands "grabbing" | **EPMs pulsing** to catch vertical traveling waves — contactless magnetic coupling |
| Goal at Kármán Line (100 km) | Placeholder — the real tether **extends past GEO (35,786 km)** to a counterweight |
| Material dropdown | Real candidate tether materials by specific strength |
| Magnet upgrades | Real magnetic-coupling tech (Alnico → Neodymium → Halbach) |
| Wave type / frequency / amplitude / tension | Real traveling-wave drive parameters on a tensioned tether |

### B.1 What the code already gets right (higher fidelity than expected)

- **Material ladder** (`Space_Monkey_Elevator.html:239-243`): Pentalayer Rhombohedral Graphene 50 GPa → Polycrystalline 100 → Monocrystalline 130 → CNT 150 → CC-CNT 300 GPa. A real specific-strength progression toward space-elevator-viable materials.
- **Magnet ladder** (`546-553`, `1182-1188`): Alnico 1.5× → Neodymium 2.5× → **Halbach Array 4.0×** — correct real-world ordering of EPM coupling strength (Halbach = one-sided flux concentration).
- **Coupling physics** (`calculateGrabMomentum`, `1422-1447`): momentum = `waveVelocity × quality × tetherMult × gripMult`. The intent — impart momentum proportional to the wave's longitudinal (axial) velocity — is physically sound, though the *optimal-phase target* is mis-set (see B.6 for the fix).
- **Wave shapes** (`596-612`): sine/square/sawtooth traveling waves; sawtooth is asymmetric (relevant — see B.2.6).
- **Tension → wave behavior** (`2283`): higher tension reduces displacement (`pos × (1 − tension×0.008)`), a rough proxy for a stiffer/tauter string.
- **Payload weight** (`1445`): `1/(1 + weight/100)` — heavier payload, less acceleration.

### B.2 Fidelity gaps between current sim and the stated concept

**B.2.1 Altitude ceiling stops at Kármán (100 km).** The defining destination of a space elevator is **GEO (35,786 km)** and beyond to a counterweight (~100,000 km). 100 km is ~0.28% of GEO. The "win" at Kármán is conceptually wrong for this sim. Real milestone set should include LEO, GEO (the force-balance point), and post-GEO.

**B.2.2 Gravity is constant (`GRAVITY: 160`, `1249`).** A space elevator's signature physics is that **effective gravity decreases with altitude and flips sign at GEO** (above GEO, centrifugal > gravity → the climber is flung *outward*). The current model pulls down with constant force everywhere — it cannot express the most important teaching moment of the whole concept.

**B.2.3 "Grab" framing contradicts the no-contact concept.** Controls/README say "grab/release the tether," and the monkey has a "grabbing" SVG pose. The actual mechanism is **contactless EPM pulsing**. Terminology and visuals undercut the very idea the sim exists to communicate.

**B.2.4 The climber's mission is inverted.** Real climber *deposits* material to strengthen the seed tether (bootstrapping). The game has the climber *collect upgrades to get stronger*. There is no mechanic for thickening the tether / improving its taper across runs.

**B.2.5 The EPM mechanism is invisible.** Nothing in-game explains that the hands are pulsing magnets coupling to a wave. The "grip" slider and magnet upgrades are the EPM model but are never framed as such.

**B.2.6 Wave-shape propulsion rationale is unexplained.** Asymmetric traveling waves (sawtooth) can produce *net directional* impulse — the same principle as traveling-wave / peristaltic motors. Whether wave-shape choice is meant to model real net-thrust asymmetry, or is purely a difficulty flavor, is undocumented.

**B.2.7 Units/scale are illustrative, not stated.** Frequency slider `0–100`, amplitude `100–1000`, tension `1–20000 kg`. Whether these are meant to map to real units (Hz, m, tether tension) or remain qualitative is unclear — matters a lot for an educational sim.

**B.2.8 Uniform tether, no taper.** Real seed/space-elevator tethers have an exponential **taper** (thickest at GEO). The sim's tether is uniform width.

> These gaps define a spectrum: keep it an approachable arcade toy, or evolve it into a defensible educational simulation. The questions below decide where on that spectrum v1.0 should land. **Resolving B is a prerequisite to finalizing Part A's framing** (README copy, milestone/win design, "self-contained" claims all depend on the answers).

### B.3 Decisions (from the author)

- **D1 — Reach: keep the Kármán (100 km) ceiling playable.** No GEO/counterweight physics in the playable climb, and **no gravity-flip in v1.0**. GEO (35,786 km), the force-balance/flip, and the counterweight become **educational context only** (explainer text / a "what's above" panel), not playable. → B.2.1 / B.2.2 are reframed as *educational content*, not physics changes.
- **D2 — Reframe to contactless EPM.** Rename "grab/release" → "pulse / couple / catch the wave" across UI, controls, README. Update the monkey so the hands read as **pulsing electro-permanent magnets** (glow/pulse), not a literal grab. Add a short in-game explainer of the contactless coupling. → resolves B.2.3 / B.2.5.
- **D3 — This game models the UP-CLIMBER only.**
  - Up-climbers are optimized for **speed and cargo** (the cargo is material destined for the tether).
  - Up-climbers **harvest energy via regenerative capture from the tether-wave momentum to power their EPMs** — a closed energy loop.
  - **Down-climbers reinforce the tether and are NOT in this game** (explicitly future / out of scope).
  - → corrects B.2.4: the mission is *carry cargo up + manage EPM energy via regen*, **not** deposit/strengthen.
- **D4 — Educational fidelity.** Real units on the sliders, real physics where tractable (longitudinal wave speed `v=√(E/ρ)`, momentum coupling), explainer panels tying settings/landmarks to the concept, and surface the **wave-shape net-thrust rationale** (asymmetric/sawtooth traveling wave ≈ traveling-wave-motor net impulse). → resolves B.2.6 / B.2.7. Taper (B.2.8) is **deferred** (relates to down-climber tether-building, out of scope).

### B.4 New mechanic to design: the EPM regen/energy loop (currently absent)

Today, pulses ("grabs") are free — there is no energy budget (`calculateGrabMomentum`, `1422`). D3 requires modeling:
- An **EPM energy reserve** that each pulse *consumes* (stronger coupling / Halbach = more draw).
- **Regenerative capture**: the climber recovers energy from wave momentum (e.g. on the deceleration/return phase, or as a fraction of captured momentum) to refill the reserve.
- A **speed ↔ cargo ↔ energy** tradeoff: more cargo (weight slider) = more energy per unit climb; the player optimizes pulse timing not just for momentum but for **net energy positive** climbing.
- This converts the existing "grip/magnet" stats and "weight" slider into a coherent power-management layer, and gives the up-climber a real reason to time pulses well beyond raw momentum.

**Open design questions for B.4 are S8–S11 below.**

### B.5 EPM regen/energy loop — resolved design

**Decisions (from author):** energy loop is a **real gated resource** (S8); **cargo is an explicit delivery objective** (S10); EPM tiers are a **coupling-strength-vs-power-draw tradeoff** (S11). Regen direction analyzed below (S9).

**Regen physics — recommendation: harvest from the UP-wave.**
- A longitudinal (compression) traveling wave moving up the tether carries energy and momentum *in the propagation direction (up)*. The tether's axial material motion couples directly to the EPM (eddy-current / Lorentz), so absorbing part of the up-wave yields an **upward** impulse — a stronger, first-order coupling than transverse radiation pressure.
- Skimming the required <10% for EPM power is **second-order** in propulsion loss but **first-order** in energy harvested → regen and propulsion are unified in a single up-wave coupling, with negligible climb penalty.
- Harvesting from **down/return waves** would absorb downward momentum (brakes the climb) — that is **regenerative braking**, the correct mechanism for the **future down-climber descending under gravity**, not the up-climber.
- Maps cleanly to the author's up/down split: **up-climber → up-wave surfing; down-climber (future) → braking regen on descent.**

**Resulting game loop (no friction; momentum conserved in vacuum):**
- Starting from rest is the hardest phase (need several good couplings to build momentum) — matches "no friction, very difficult to start vertical motion." In cruise you only pulse to beat gravity + accelerate.
- A pulse **couples to the up-wave → gains upward momentum (propulsion)** and **skims ~5–10% as EPM charge (regen)**.
- **EPM charge** is the gated resource: pulsing costs charge (stronger tiers cost more), good couplings refill it. The *real* limiting cost is **propulsion momentum**; EPM control power is small but can **brown out** on repeated poorly-timed pulses (charge spent, little momentum returned) → can't pulse → coast/fall.
- **Cargo** raises momentum needed per unit altitude (heavier) and charge throughput; delivery objective = "get N kg to 100 km."
- **EPM tiers:** Alnico (weak pull, low draw) → Neodymium → Halbach (strongest pull, highest draw) — a real strategic tradeoff, not a strict upgrade.

### B.5.1 EPM-charge UI & tuning (deep-dive)

The EPM charge is now the central resource, so it gets **both** a stable readout and diegetic reinforcement. The existing chest **badge** (`renderMonkey`, `2517-2602`) moves with the climber and is hard to read mid-fall, so the charge meter must NOT live only on the badge.

**Display (two layers):**
1. **Fixed gauge (primary):** a vertical battery-style bar at a fixed screen corner (pairs naturally with the fixed top-right altitude readout from Phase 4 / REVIEW). Gradient green→amber→red by level; shows **% + a net-flow arrow** (▲ green when regen ≥ drain, ▼ red when net-draining) so players *learn the timing→energy link*. This is the one always-legible element.
2. **Diegetic (juice + reframe):** the **EPM hands glow**, brightness/pulse-rate ∝ charge; they **redden/flicker near brownout** and go dark at zero. Reinforces "the hands are pulsing magnets" (D2) without reading the gauge.
3. **Brownout state:** gauge flashes red, hands dark, low warning tone (audio 3.1), on-canvas hint "EPM BROWNOUT — coast to recharge."

**Tuning (initial values; flag for playtest — capacity normalized 0–100):**
- **Drain per pulse:** Alnico 4 · Neodymium 8 · Halbach 15 (tier = strength-vs-draw, S11).
- **Regen per coupling:** `qualityFactor(0..1) × waveEnergyFactor(altitude) × tierGain` — so a *well-timed* pulse is net-positive, a *poorly-timed* one is net-negative. Ties B.6 peak-velocity timing directly to energy survival, not just momentum.
- **Passive trickle:** small ambient eddy-current regen (~+1.5/s) only while near the tether, so brownout is **recoverable by coasting** (supports S14 brownout = soft-fail).
- **Brownout:** charge ≤ 0 → pulses yield no momentum; re-enable above ~15%.
- **Emergent ceiling (ties to Q-P3):** because `waveEnergyFactor` falls with altitude, regen-per-coupling drops with height; eventually **even perfect timing goes net-negative → you can't climb further** unless a stronger material (less attenuation) raises that ceiling. **The charge sustainability curve *is* the difficulty curve** — material choice sets how high you can go. Cargo taxes it indirectly (more pulses needed per metre → more total drain).

### B.6 Coupling-timing fidelity bug (found during analysis)

`calculateGrabMomentum` (`1422-1447`) sets `optimalPhase = 0.25` (`1425`) — the sine **crest** (max displacement, axial velocity ≈ 0) — yet computes `momentum = waveVelocity × quality` where `waveVelocity = amp·ω·cos` is ≈ 0 at that exact phase (`599`). The model rewards timing to the moment of *least* wave velocity while multiplying by ~zero velocity: internally inconsistent. Physically, peak **momentum flux** is at the **zero-crossing** (peak axial/longitudinal velocity). For the educational sim, retarget optimal coupling to **peak wave velocity**, so the timing skill matches the physics and the regen/propulsion model in B.5.

### B.7 Wave-shape physics — net thrust & teaching value

Net propulsion from a traveling wave comes from **either** gated coupling on a symmetric wave **or** an asymmetric waveform that yields net impulse without gating (the **traveling-wave / peristaltic / ratchet motor** principle). The three existing shapes map onto a real skill/physics gradient (velocity profiles at `596-612`):

| Shape | Velocity profile (code) | Physical reading | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sine** | smooth `amp·ω·cos` | symmetric — net thrust requires **gated pulsing** (couple up-phase, release down-phase) | forgiving, wide window; learning/cruise |
| **Sawtooth** | long slow drift + sharp `×50` snap (`607-610`) | **asymmetric ratchet** → models net **directional** traveling-wave thrust | narrow window, high reward; efficient climb |
| **Square** | zero except `×100` edge spikes (`603`) | bang-bang impulsive coupling | extreme peak, tiny window; high skill |

Teaching point to surface in an explainer: *why* an asymmetric wave can drive net transport without timing, while a sine needs the climber to pulse. **Option:** give sawtooth a small *continuous* net assist (true ratchet behavior) rather than only changing the pulse window — bigger code change; decide appetite (S12).

### B.8 Real-units chain — couple the sliders into one physics model

The sliders already display real units (`232-274`): Material GPa, Frequency Hz, Amplitude mm, Width mm (tether diameter), Tension kgf, Gravity G (0.01–1, already supports reduced-G), Weight kg (climber+cargo). They are **not physically connected** — e.g. material only sets a damping fudge (`2113`), tension a display fudge (`2283`). The educational v1.0 win is to wire them into one consistent chain. **Note (B.13): the wave is longitudinal, so its speed is `v = √(E/ρ)` (material stiffness/density), not the transverse `√(T/μ)`** — tension does not set propagation speed for a compression wave.

```
material Young's modulus E + density ρ      → wave speed  v = √(E/ρ)   [displayed, ~material constant]
width (diameter) + material density         → linear mass density μ
material tensile strength × cross-section   → max safe tension (break limit)
tension T + μ                               → coupling-momentum modifier (tauter tether couples better)
v + amplitude + frequency                   → available momentum/energy flux per pulse
weight/cargo + gravity                      → resistance to climb
```

Effects:
- **Material gains a real role:** stronger material → higher safe tension and (via attenuation, Q-P3) greater reach. Over-tension (T > strength×area) **breaks the tether** (a real, teachable fail state) instead of today's cosmetic damping.
- **Tension's honest role** is keeping the tether taut so the compression wave propagates and couples well — modelled as a coupling-momentum modifier, not as the wave's speed.
- **Derived readouts** (wave speed, tension headroom, momentum-per-pulse) make the sim legible and defensible.
- Reuses existing knobs/units — mostly *connecting* them, plus a few display fields.
Decide fidelity appetite (S13): full chain with break-limit, or a lighter "wave speed readout + sensible coupling" subset.

### B.9 Educational explainer content

Landmarks already carry `info` strings (e.g. `455`, `495`, `531`). Extend the educational layer:
- Per-setting "ⓘ" explainers tying each knob to the concept (material ladder → why CNT/graphene; tension → wave speed; wave shape → ratchet propulsion; EPM tiers → Halbach flux).
- A **"what's above 100 km" panel**: since the playable ceiling stays at Kármán (D1), use the win/finish screen to teach that the *real* seed tether continues to **GEO (35,786 km)** and a counterweight beyond, where effective gravity flips — the part the game doesn't simulate but the concept requires.
- Frame the **up-climber vs down-climber** roles and the **regen/EPM** loop in a short concept blurb.

**Remaining design questions: S12 (sawtooth net-thrust modeling) and S13 (units-chain fidelity) below.**

### B.12 Physics spine — coupling mechanism & wave energy source (open)

The sim never states *what the EPM couples to* or *where the wave energy originates*. Locking these makes the educational layer (D4) defensible and may yield emergent gameplay:

- **Coupling mechanism (Q-P1):** a graphene/CNT tether is **not ferromagnetic**, so a magnet can't "grip" it directly. Carbon is **conductive**, so the physical fit is **eddy-current / linear-induction coupling** — a pulsed EPM induces eddy currents in the moving tether → contactless Lorentz force. Alternatives: embedded ferromagnetic striping, or a magnetic ribbon tether.
- **Wave energy source (Q-P2):** waves must be **driven**. Most elegant: a **ground/anchor oscillator beams mechanical wave-power up the tether**; the frequency/amplitude/shape/tension sliders are that driver's controls. The climber is **fuel-less**, harvesting beamed wave power (and skimming <10% for EPMs). Alternatives: self-excited climber, or ambient/natural excitation.
- **Emergent difficulty (Q-P3):** if waves are driven from below, they **attenuate with altitude** (tether damping). Higher → less wave energy → harder; **stiffer/stronger material attenuates less** → material choice gains real gameplay weight and a physically-motivated difficulty ramp (better than the arbitrary ramp in Phase 4).

These resolve the "how does it actually work" spine and feed B.7/B.8/B.9 (explainers, units chain, wave model). **Questions Q-P1/Q-P2/Q-P3 below.**

**B.12 resolved — the conceptual spine:**
- **Q-P1: eddy-current / induction coupling.** Pulsed EPMs induce eddy currents in the conductive carbon tether → contactless Lorentz force. Coupling strength ∝ tether conductivity × **slip** (wave phase velocity − climber velocity) × EPM field strength. EPM tiers (Alnico→Neodymium→Halbach) = field strength vs power draw (S11).
- **Q-P2: ground-driven beamed wave-power.** A ground/anchor oscillator launches traveling waves up the tether; the **frequency / amplitude / wave-shape / tension** sliders are that driver's controls. The climber is **fuel-less** — it harvests beamed wave power, skimming <10% for its EPMs (B.5).
- **Q-P3: waves attenuate with altitude.** Tether damping reduces wave energy with height; **stiffer/stronger material attenuates less**. Higher = harder, and **material choice becomes the primary determinant of reachable altitude** — a physically-motivated difficulty ramp that **replaces the arbitrary ramp** in Phase 4.

**One-paragraph spine (for README/explainers):** *A ground station beams longitudinal (compression) waves up a conductive graphene/CNT space-elevator seed tether. An up-climber rides them contactlessly: its electro-permanent-magnet hands pulse to induce eddy currents in the moving tether, coupling at peak wave velocity to gain upward momentum while regeneratively skimming <10% of that energy to power the magnets. It carries cargo (tether material) toward the Kármán Line. Waves fade with altitude, so a stronger tether material reaches higher. Down-climbers — which would brake regeneratively on descent and reinforce the tether — are the next chapter.*

### B.13 Wave orientation — longitudinal, not transverse (resolved)

**Decision (from author): the tether carries a LONGITUDINAL (compression) wave.** This aligns the
physics, the visuals, and the copy, which had drifted into disagreement.

- **What the code already drew:** the tether renderer (`Space_Monkey_Elevator.html:3074`) builds a
  per-segment phase with vertical offsets, compression/rarefaction, and brightness/width varying in
  the compressed zones — commented "creates traveling compression wave." The on-screen tether was
  *already* longitudinal; the words and the formula were not.
- **What was wrong:** the wave-speed readout used `v = √(T/μ)` (the transverse/string speed), the
  coupling comment said "transverse," and the settings copy said the wave "swings" and the ground
  station "shakes" the tether. With the default sliders `√(T/μ)` ≈ 19 m/s; the correct longitudinal
  speed `√(E/ρ)` ≈ **23.6 km/s** (E ≈ 1 TPa, ρ = 1800 kg/m³) — a ~700× difference, and exactly the
  kind of contradiction a technical reader would seize on.
- **Why longitudinal is the stronger concept:** the tether's *axial* material motion couples directly
  to upward impulse via eddy-current/Lorentz — first-order, unlike the second-order transverse
  radiation-pressure argument B.5 previously relied on. And "the pulse arrives at ~23.6 km/s, faster
  than orbital velocity" is a far better teaching hook: the climber rides the wave, it doesn't chase it.
- **Code changes made:** `tetherWaveSpeed()` now returns the honest longitudinal speed `√(E/ρ)`
  (a material-independent constant, since E ~ 1 TPa and density are ~constant across the carbon
  ladder) for the settings readout, displayed in km/s. The tension/width → momentum link is preserved
  as a clearly-labelled **gameplay coupling-momentum modifier** (`couplingMomentumScale`), not the
  wave's propagation speed. Config gains `YOUNGS_MODULUS`. Tests updated (`tetherWaveSpeed` is now the
  longitudinal constant; `couplingMomentumScale` carries the tension/width behaviour).
- **Consequence for the tension slider:** tension no longer sets wave speed (physically correct for a
  compression wave). Its role is keeping the tether taut so the wave propagates and couples — reflected
  in the B.8 chain and the slider hint. A future fidelity option (S13) is a tether-break fail state
  from the strength ladder, which gives tension a second, teachable role.

### B.14 Vacuum-correct drag — density-scaled aero + eddy-current braking (resolved)

**Decision (from author): Option 1 with release-to-coast.** Aerodynamic drag scales with air density
and vanishes in the upper climb; eddy-current magnetic drag (∝B²σv, needs no air) limits speed while
the EPMs are engaged; releasing latches the magnets off at zero power, so the climber coasts
frictionlessly. Gravity unchanged — constant is correct (−3.1% over 100 km).

- **What was wrong:** `AIR_DRAG` removed 60% of velocity per second identically at sea level and at
  100 km, where real density is 0.00005% of sea level. Releasing at 80 km with 100 m/s left −9.9 m/s
  after 3 s; B.14 leaves +51.7 m/s, still climbing.
- **Why eddy braking is the right governor:** drag was the *only* speed limiter, so removing it
  naively produced absurd speeds. Eddy braking needs no atmosphere, and because coupling force and
  eddy drag both scale with field, top speed becomes induction-motor *slip* — set by the wave
  (amplitude × frequency, the ground station's controls) rather than by the magnets, which set
  acceleration only.
- **Measured:** peak speed collapses to a stable ~770 km/h across all grip/tier combinations (today:
  679–4,885 km/h) and full climbs settle at 10–12 min (today: 5.3–22.5 min).
- **Sea-level feel preserved:** aero and eddy are a multiplicative split of the tuned `AIR_DRAG`,
  exact at reference field. `EDDY_FRACTION = 0.08`, flagged for playtest.
- **Linearization:** the synchronous speed of a traveling-field linear induction motor is the wave's
  **phase** velocity (`√(E/ρ)` ≈ 23.6 km/s), **not** the tether's material velocity (`A·ω` ≈ 79 km/h
  at current settings). The climber therefore runs at very high **slip** behind the field pattern, and
  ~770 km/h is an unremarkable operating point rather than a violation. `EDDY_FRACTION` is an arcade
  stand-in that **linearises the slip curve** (real induction torque is non-linear in slip). This
  **retires the B.2.7 contradiction** for the coupling model; the amplitude/frequency *display* units
  remain illustrative (B.2.7).

### B.15 Aero-kit drag multiplier applied to the wrong term (fixed)

`dragMult` (Aero 0.95, Streamline 0.9) multiplied the drag **retention** factor rather than the drag
**loss**, so the kits made drag far worse: both kits retained 0.000033 of velocity per second (vs
0.4038 unequipped) and made the 100 km climb unreachable. Now applied to the loss term, so the kits
reduce drag as intended (1.17× sea-level terminal velocity with both). Accumulation logic unchanged.
Note that under B.14 the kits are minor by construction, since aero drag only exists low down —
revisit their magnitudes, or repurpose them against eddy drag, if they should feel stronger.
